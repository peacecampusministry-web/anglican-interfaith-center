Anglican Faith and Student Transformation

Anglican Student Transformation Ministry helps university students explore Christian faith on a religiously diverse campus. Its approach draws on Anglican worship, study, and service, and makes room for honest conversation with people of other traditions.

The Anglican Church in North America

The Anglican Church in North America (ACNA) was formed in 2009. It is rooted in the Anglican tradition and emphasizes the authority of Scripture, historic Christian teaching, and worship shaped by the Book of Common Prayer. Anglican Christianity has historical roots in the Church of England and is practiced around the world.

Belief and worship

Anglicans confess one God in three persons and believe that Jesus Christ is fully divine and fully human. Scripture guides faith and practice, and the historic creeds summarize the Church’s faith. Bishops serve in the historic ministry of the Church.

Liturgy is the shared pattern of worship. In Holy Communion, the service includes readings, preaching, and prayers, followed by the celebration of the sacrament. Anglicans recognize Baptism and Holy Communion as sacraments instituted by Christ. The Book of Common Prayer gives structure to public worship and personal prayer.

Principles for interfaith dialogue

  • Respect human dignity. Approach each person with humility and give them room to speak for themselves.
  • Speak from Christian faith. Share Anglican convictions openly, including the confession of Jesus Christ in John 14:6.
  • Learn from one another. Ask sincere questions and listen to how people describe their own traditions.
  • Work together where possible. Look for common concerns, such as serving neighbors, supporting refugees, and caring for the environment.
  • Respond to local needs. Shape conversations around the people and concerns in each setting.
  • Pursue peace and reconciliation. Build relationships that can withstand disagreement and make cooperation possible.

Student Transformation Ministry

The ministry helps university students explore how Christian faith informs their studies, friendships, and life on a diverse campus. Students can discuss science and faith, grow through prayer and Bible study, and receive encouragement through mentorship. Daily prayer, weekly worship, and regular Communion support their spiritual life. Service and interfaith projects give students opportunities to work with peers from different religious backgrounds on shared concerns.
